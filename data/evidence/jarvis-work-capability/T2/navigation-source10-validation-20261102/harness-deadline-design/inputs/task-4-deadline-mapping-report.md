# Task 4 decision support — 315-second outer seal

**Disposition for the reviewed bytes: hold execution.** A single caller-owned deadline can govern admission to timed I/O and prove that one successful case finished within its allowance. Neither that mapping nor any finite reservation proves that the parent, all descendants and the final filesystem write will always finish by second 315. If the latter is the release requirement, the constraints conflict. Do not approve the present checked-after-cleanup implementation for either interpretation.

## Binding and time origin

Design lines 13, 44, 64, 66 and 75 specify the service-entry clock, first-outcome freeze, existing 300-second client and “outer 315 s sealing, no renewal”, explicit service admission/observation semantics and stopping on the first deadline or missing confirmation. Implementation plan lines 144, 147–150 bind real P4 ownership/cleanup, the inert 315-second seal and actual 260/300/315 maxima with zero retries. The text does not specify an interpreter-start timestamp or promise preemption of an arbitrary blocked kernel call. The service caveat is not an exemption for late outer evidence. Old P1 line 16 and 165–169 give a CLI wait of 315; old P4 lines 503–506 and 516–535 do further work after that wait. They supply reusable primitives, not a whole-case timing proof.

Use invocation of the parent case entry, before *any* case-specific admission I/O, as the conservative outer origin (a proposed operational definition). Include authority/hash/proof/port checks, exclusive claim, startup, CLI launch/discovery, work, packet and file validation, effects, cleanup, protected/input readback and sealing of all required evidence. Separate root allocation and later independent semantic adjudication are outside this execution clock. If root measures the command from before interpreter creation, honor that earlier origin. The 120-second authority validity and 60-second fresh baseline remain independent admission tests. Start the client's 300-second maximum by dispatch, including time in launch/discovery when computing the parent wait. Product service still starts 260 at service entry. A child must never issue a new 315-second lease.

Adapter lines 117–135, 148–165, 177–204 and 292–308 and headless lines 8–39 omit admission/claim from the clock, allow readiness observations past the local readiness limit, and test elapsed before terminal serialization/close. Actual line 202 additionally hashes files after the check. A pre-write timestamp cannot establish timely publication. A late success-shaped terminal cannot be authoritative.

## Counterexample to reservation with unchanged waits

P4 lines 150–205, 208–246 and 248–259, P1 lines 75–91 and 100–116. For N distinct known identities, L launchers, no fixture and no console auxiliary, configured cleanup wait components are 15 + (15+10+15)N + 2L + 15 + 1 = 31 + 40N + 2L seconds. This is a sum of available wait components, not a wall-time bound. Two roots alone give 115 seconds, leaving at most 200 before cleanup even with zero admission/validation cost. Four identities with two launchers give 195, leaving 120. An auxiliary can add nearly 3+15+.05 seconds of observations and sleep before the already-counted final snapshot. Fixture prewait would add 3 seconds per fixture; this pair of paths uses none. New matching children can be discovered during cleanup. An assumed N=4 is not an invariant; refusing to inspect or truncating the set at four would discard effects. Arbitrary blocking in launch/files/identity is sufficient even for N=1. Reserving only 15 after a 300-second wait cannot cover even the two fixed snapshots at configured maxima. Reserving the full sum for a guessed tree takes away much of the usable service window without solving the problem.

## Recommended decision and minimal operational mapping

Record explicitly in the Task 4 release criteria that receipt eligibility expires at D=t0+315 without action by a blocked worker. Require **positive, timely evidence** of the entire seal for any candidate pass. Missing, uncertain or late completion irrevocably fails this allocation. An already admitted operation may return later, and residual processes/effects require separate root recovery. This distinguishes a deadline on successful completion from guaranteed completion of cleanup on every failure. It preserves all three maxima; it gives no authority for running a case or admitting recovery after D. If root requires physical completion of every failure path by D, retain the conflict and withhold release. No in-process timer, thread timeout or parent kill supplies that guarantee, and a suspended parent at D is already a counterexample. A smaller numerical time cap cannot fix it. This explicit receipt criterion is the smallest plan clarification/revision proposed here; if a hard process-stop guarantee is indispensable, it does not satisfy it.

Subject to that ruling, use one per-invocation monotonic_ns deadline in V/harness/adapter.py, passed through run_case→admit→execute_case and the analogous V/harness/headless_proof.py main→inert_admit path. Bind child checks to the same origin via authenticated/verified parent attempt data and a same-host same-clock reading; reject stale/missing binding. Keep the public run_case signature. Use distinct earlier work and verification cutoffs so residual capacity is actually available for cleanup. For concrete preparation testing, use D−40 s for end of work/normal validation and D−5 s for end of cleanup. These are proposed explicit scheduling reservations, not worst-case bounds or runtime evidence. With 12 seconds spent before dispatch, at most 263 seconds remain until D−40 for launch, client and pre-cleanup validation; with 30 spent, at most 245. The full 300-second client wait is never available under that policy; effective maximum is 275 less startup/validation, while product limits stay 300/260. The authority/review must expose these effective limits, not hide them in an unchanged budget dictionary. Move to cleanup promptly on first failure, reserve first-result/effects capture before cleanup and reserve post-cleanup readback/terminal within D. If startup consumes the productive window or cleanup cannot be established, decline dispatch. Timely success is possible for fast cases; no arithmetic demonstrates suitability for the original 8B case. Inert timing is evidence only for inert. No change to weights, product deadlines or default behavior.

One 35-second cleanup slice does **not** guarantee even four full native waits; an exhausted slice can leave an owned server alive. This is a material recovery cost and must be explicit before allocation.

For timely-seal evidence use an enclosing observation in the existing root invocation/receipt flow: record the monotonic start before calling/launching the harness, then the monotonic return/observation **after** the immutable terminal and all required data closed, binding exact run ID, terminal digest and clock domain. This is a receipt observation, not an ownership controller. No valid observation ⇒ permanently ineligible; later diagnostics/recovery are separately labeled and do not promote the attempt. If an enclosing observation is unavailable, no timed PASS. Guard success on a clean packet/readback, proven closure and five exact native protected identities, independent of that timing witness. First raw CLI/service result and first failure remain exclusive and unmodified, known side effects retained, native starts null unless proved, no retry, no next case. Clean exit alone never suffices.
